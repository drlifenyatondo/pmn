PrEP vs. PEP: Understanding the Key Differences

Purity M. Nyatondo 3 min read HIV Facts

PrEP and PEP both prevent HIV in people who are currently HIV-negative, yet they are used at completely different times. The easiest way to remember the difference is in the first letter: Pre means before, and Post means after.

Side-by-Side Comparison

Feature PrEP (Pre-Exposure) PEP (Post-Exposure)
When it is taken Before potential exposure to HIV After a possible exposure to HIV
Urgency Ongoing planned protection Emergency — must start within 72 hours
Duration Taken for as long as risk continues Taken daily for 28 to 30 days
Available forms Daily pills, vaginal ring, or injections (CAB-LA, Lenacapavir) Oral pills taken daily

Moving from PEP to PrEP

If you ever need PEP after an emergency exposure, you can talk to your nurse or doctor about transitioning straight onto PrEP once your 28-day PEP course is complete so you stay protected going forward.
