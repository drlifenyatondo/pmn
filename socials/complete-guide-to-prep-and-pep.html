Your Complete Guide to PrEP Choices, PEP & Clinic Access

Purity M. Nyatondo 6 min read Complete Guide

Protecting yourself from HIV has never offered more choice. Whether you want ongoing protection before an exposure or emergency protection after an unexpected risk, understanding how PrEP and PEP work empowers you to stay in control of your health.

1. What is PrEP & What Choices Are Available?

PrEP (Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis) is medication taken by HIV-negative people before potential exposure to prevent HIV. Today, there are four main PrEP options designed to fit different lifestyles:

  • Oral PrEP: A daily pill (or event-driven 2-1-1 dosing for eligible men) that stops HIV from establishing an infection.
  • PrEP Ring (Dapivirine Vaginal Ring): A flexible monthly silicone ring inserted in the vagina for 28 days of discreet, woman-controlled protection.
  • Cabotegravir (CAB-LA): A long-acting injection given once every two months after two initial monthly doses.
  • Lenacapavir: A long-acting injection administered just twice a year (every six months).

2. What is PEP & Why Does Timing Matter?

PEP (Post-Exposure Prophylaxis) is emergency HIV prevention medication taken after a possible exposure (such as unprotected sex, a broken condom, shared needles, or sexual assault).

  • Start within 72 hours: You must begin PEP as soon as possible—and no later than 72 hours (3 days) after exposure.
  • Complete the 28–30 day course: You must take your PEP pills every single day for 28 to 30 days without skipping doses.

3. Where to Get PrEP and PEP

You can access PrEP and PEP at your nearest public clinic, hospital, youth-friendly health center, or sexual and reproductive health clinic (such as New Start Centres and partner NGO clinics across Zimbabwe). Services are confidential, and a healthcare worker will provide a quick HIV test, answer your questions, and help you choose the option that works best for you.

4. Key Reminders for Total Protection

  • HIV testing comes first: Both PrEP and PEP are strictly for people who test HIV-negative.
  • Combine with condoms: PrEP and PEP prevent HIV, while condoms add extra protection against other STIs and unintended pregnancy.
  • No judgment: Asking for PrEP or PEP is a responsible, proactive step toward protecting your future.
