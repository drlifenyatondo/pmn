PEP: Emergency HIV Prevention Within 72 Hours

Purity M. Nyatondo 4 min read Emergency Prevention

PEP stands for Post-Exposure Prophylaxis. It is an emergency course of antiretroviral medicines taken after a possible exposure to HIV to stop the virus from taking hold in your body.

The 72-Hour Golden Rule

Timing is everything with PEP. You must start taking PEP within 72 hours (3 days) of a potential exposure—and the sooner you start, the better it works. Every hour counts.

When Should Someone Seek PEP?

  • A condom broke, slipped off, or was not used during sex with someone whose HIV status is unknown or who is HIV-positive without viral suppression.
  • Sharing needles or syringes.
  • Following sexual assault or a needle-stick injury at work.

Completing the 28 to 30-Day Course

Once prescribed at a clinic or hospital, PEP must be taken every single day for 28 to 30 days. Stopping early reduces its effectiveness. After finishing the course, a healthcare provider will conduct follow-up HIV testing to confirm you remain HIV-negative.
